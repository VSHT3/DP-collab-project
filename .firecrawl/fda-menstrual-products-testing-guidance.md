II. Background

In the fiscal year 2022 and 2023 appropriations reports from the House Appropriations
2,3
Committee, the Committee requested that FDA update its existing guidance on tampons and
pads, “Menstrual Tampons and Pads: Information for Premarket Notification Submissions
(510(k)s),” issued on July 27, 2005. These reports focused largely on recommendations related to
menstrual product materials (e.g., disclosing ingredients and evaluating contaminants), reflecting
growing public interest for increased transparency about the materials used to make tampons. In
addition, FDA is aware of concerns about the potential release of metals from tampons after a
4
2024 study reported metals in tampons during laboratory testing. However, the methods used in
the 2024 study did not allow for estimation of toxicological risks associated with tampons, as
there was no consideration of the amount of metals released from the tampon materials. As a
result, FDA commissioned an independent, systematic literature review to evaluate the metal
content in tampons sold in the United States (US) and the amount of metals which might
thereafter be absorbed by users. Although limitations were identified in the systematic literature
review, as is common, the findings did not identify safety concerns associated with tampon use
5
and contaminant exposure.

This draft guidance, which when final will supersede “Menstrual Tampons and Pads:
Information for Premarket Notification Submissions (510(k)s),” includes information requested
by the Committee in these appropriations reports and provides additional detail and clarifications
to existing recommendations that are intended to enhance transparency and consistency of
menstrual product labeling and performance testing. Specifically, the draft guidance includes
recommendations for:

• menstrual cups;

• disclosing ingredients (including fragrances/deodorants) on all menstrual product outer
package labels;

• evaluating contaminants for all menstrual products;

VI. Performance Testing Recommendations

Pursuant to section 502(f) of the FD&C Act and 21 CFR 801.5, a device must have labeling that
bears adequate directions for use, meaning directions under which the layman can use a device
safely and for the purposes for which it is intended. Due to the nature and duration of contact of
menstrual products, in order for the device to be used safely when used as intended, we
recommend the performance testing discussed in this section to evaluate the safety of the
materials and performance characteristics of the device. Such testing will help to inform what is
needed to ensure, among other things, the adequacy of the directions for use included in the
labeling. Such recommended performance testing, and documentation of such testing, will also
help to ensure compliance with the QSR (21 CFR Part 820) and record requirements to the extent
required under applicable law, including in the QSR (e.g., 21 CFR 820.30(j)). Notably, QSR
records and other records required under the FD&C Act must generally be made available to an
FDA investigator upon request (see section 704(e) of the FD&C Act).

Biocompatibility

Significance: Tampons contain patient-contacting materials, which, when used for their intended
purpose, (i.e., contact type and duration), may induce a harmful biological response.

Recommendation: You should determine the biocompatibility of all patient-contacting
component materials present in your tampon. If your tampon is identical in chemical
composition, manufacturing and processing methods to a tampon of the same type with a history
of safe use, you may reference previous testing experience or the literature, if appropriate. For
some device materials, it may be appropriate to provide a reference to either a recognized
consensus standard, or to an LOA for a MAF. You should refer to the FDA’s Device Master
Files webpage for additional information on using MAFs.
