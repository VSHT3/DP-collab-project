Menstrual Products: Health Risks and Safety Regulations
This document summarizes scientifically supported risks associated with menstrual products and compares the
products analyzed in the research review.
1. Main Health Risks of Menstrual Products
Bacterial Growth
Tampons carry the highest bacterial growth risk because they introduce oxygen into the vagina, which can
support Staphylococcus aureus growth. Pads have lower risk but prolonged wear can create a warm, moist
environment for bacteria.

Toxic Shock Syndrome (TSS)
TSS is strongly associated with tampon use, especially high-absorbency tampons. Pads do not carry TSS risk
because they are external products.

2. FDA and Safety Regulations
In the United States, tampons, menstrual cups, and scented pads are regulated by the FDA as Class Il medical
devices, while unscented pads are Class I devices. Manufacturers must follow FDA safety standards and labeling
requirements, especially for tampon absorbency.
However, the FDA does not currently require routine testing for toxic chemicals such as heavy metals or PFAS in
menstrual products.
Recent scientific studies found heavy metals such as lead and arsenic in tampons and PFAS ('forever
chemicals') in some reusable menstrual products. Because of these findings, states such as California, Vermont,
and New York have introduced or passed laws restricting PFAS
