# CS Presentation Script

---

## 1. Introduction (30 sec)

Our collaborative science project investigated menstrual products across four disciplines — Biology, Chemistry, Physics, and Environmental Science. My role was Computer Science: I built this website to tie everything together and make the findings accessible.

---

## 2. Home Page (20 sec)

The home page introduces the project. Our research question asks which type of menstrual product best minimizes health risks and environmental impact. Five evaluation axes, one per subject, drive the analysis.

---

## 3. Environmental Science (45 sec)

Environmental Science is where we see the biggest numbers. A Harvard survey of over 7,000 people shows nearly half use regular tampons and pads — those are single-use plastics. Globally, about 80 billion products are used per menstrual cycle. In the UK alone, 4.3 billion per year. These take about 800 years to decompose in landfills. Two billion get flushed down UK toilets yearly, and 18,000 wash up on beaches, killing roughly 100,000 marine animals each year from plastic and chemical exposure.

The "What Can We Do" section gives practical answers: use menstrual cups, reusable pads, period pants. Never flush. Choose applicator-free tampons and organic cotton where possible. The per-product table breaks down each product's main environmental issue — commercial pads are the worst for plastic waste, reusable cloth is the best with far less overall waste.

---

## 4. Data & Results (45 sec)

The Data page has two radar charts. The overall scores radar shows how products compare across Safety, Comfort, Performance, Environment, and Cost. The specific measurements radar drills into raw sub-metrics — TSS risk, chemical exposure, skin comfort, absorption capacity and rate, annual cost.

Below that, the score breakdown bar charts let you compare products on each axis individually. The correlation explorer is particularly useful: pick two metrics and it shows their relationship. For example, Performance versus Environment shows a negative correlation of about negative 0.6 — products that absorb better tend to be worse for the planet. That's a genuine trade-off we found. There are preset buttons to quickly explore interesting relationships like cost versus performance or capacity versus absorption rate.

The rankings table lets you sort products by any axis, and the statistical summary shows mean, min, max, and standard deviation across all products.

---

## 5. Products (20 sec)

The Products page organizes all seven tested products by category — commercial pads, organic pads, reusable cloth, tampons. Each product card shows its scores across the five compound axes, plus the price and absorbency levels. The brand coverage matrix shows which brands offer which product types. Clicking any product takes you to a detail page with a radar chart and full score breakdown.

---

## 6. Recommendation Tool (25 sec)

The recommendation tool is the interactive centerpiece. You set how much each axis matters to you using sliders, and it instantly ranks all seven products. There are four preset buttons: Balanced, Health First, Performance, and Eco and Budget — each produces a different winner. The top three matches are shown as color-coded cards, and a full ranking with score bars follows below. A score breakdown reveals exactly how the winning product earned its rank.

---

## 7. Conclusions (20 sec)

The Conclusions page summarizes what we found. Jessa Cloth pad wins overall due to perfect environment and cost scores, strong safety and comfort. Jessa Cotton is the best single-use alternative. No product is perfect — you trade performance for sustainability, or safety for cost. The proposed improved product section describes what an ideal product would combine: organic cotton top layer, high-absorbency core with fast wicking, biodegradable shell, PFAS-free waterproofing.
