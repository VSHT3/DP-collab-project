Sanitary tampons: Absorb menstrual blood directly within the body. The greatest chemical risk lies in the fact that vaginal epithelial tissue is more capable of absorbing chemicals than skin. If the tampon itself contains harmful substances, the absorption risk will increase accordingly. Additionally, if used for an extended period, it provides a breeding ground for bacteria (such as Staphylococcus aureus), potentially leading to toxic shock syndrome (TSS), which is a serious bacterial infection rather than a purely chemical issue.
Sanitary napkins: Absorb externally and lock menstrual blood in the core layer through high-molecular resins, keeping the surface of the body dry. The main chemical risk lies in the fact that menstrual blood, sweat, and body temperature create a warm and humid environment. If the sanitary napkin itself has excessive bacterial colonies or contains irritating chemicals, it is more likely to cause contact dermatitis or gynecological inflammation on the external genital skin.


* Endocrine disruptors (phthalates, parabens, BPA)
    → affect hormones, fertility, cancer risk
    → found especially in fragranced products
    * PubMed study-https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41666673/
* Phthalates & VOCs (e.g., toluene)
    → linked to hormone disruption & toxicity
    → released from plastics, fragrances
    * Phthalates study-https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0890623818302259?utm
* Heavy metals (lead, arsenic)
    → toxic, accumulate in body
    → found in tampons
    * Summary article-https://www.lemonde.fr/en/environment/article/2024/07/12/toxic-metals-found-in-tampons_6682209_114.html?utm
* PFAS (“forever chemicals”)
    → linked to cancer & hormone disruption
    → used for leak-proof layers
    * PFAS overview-https://time.com/6254060/pfas-period-chemicals-underwear-tampons/?utm
