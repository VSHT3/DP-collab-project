## Physics

**Materials:**

- 4 types of products (pads, tampons)
- Digital scale (0.0/0.01g precision)
- Stopwatch (phone)
- Gloves
- _Paper towels_
- Stirring rod
- Measuring cylinder/ Beaker
- _Book_
- _Tray/flat board_
- Tap water
- Corn syrup
- Red food coloring

**Fluid preparation protocol:**

1.  Pour 90mL water into the measuring cylinder/beaker
2.  Add 10mL corn syrup (ratio 1:9)
3.  Add 2–3 drops red food coloring
4.  Stir with plastic spoon until well mixed

### Experiment 1: Absorption capacity experiment

To measure how much fluid each product holds relative to its own weight (g/g)

Approximately 200ml of menstrual fluid needed

Procedure:

1.  Put on gloves.
2.  Place a paper towel on your workspace
3.  Weigh the dry product
4.  **Record as Dry mass (g) in Table 1**
5.  Place the product flat on the paper towel
6.  Slowly pour simulated fluid using syringe onto the center of the product. (or directly from measuring cylinder)
7.  Stop pouring when fully saturated (fluid starts to pool on surface or run off edges)
8.  Record total volume poured (mL) (1 mL fluid ≈ 1 g)
9.  Carefully lift the wet product. Let excess drip for 5 seconds
10. Weigh the wet product.
11. Record as Wet mass (g) in Table 1
12. Dispose of used product in Ziploc bag (or anything else)
13. Repeat steps 2–10 for all 4 products
14. Clean workspace

Calculations:

Fluid absorbed (g) = Wet mass (g) – Dry mass (g)

Absorption capacity (g/g) = Fluid absorbed (g) ÷ Dry mass (g)

### Experiment 2: Absorption rate

To measure how fast each product absorbs 5 mL of fluid

Approximately 50ml of menstrual fluid needed

Procedure:

1.  Put on gloves
2.  Place product flat on a paper towel
3.  Measure exactly 5 mL of simulated fluid in the measuring cylinder
4.  Pour fluid using syringe onto the center of the product
5.  Start stopwatch immediately
6.  Stop stopwatch when fluid is fully absorbed (no shiny/wet surface visible)
7.  Record time (seconds) in Table 2
8.  Dispose of used product in Ziploc bag (or anything else)
9.  Repeat steps 2–8 for all 4 products
10. Repeat 3 times per product for an average
11. Clean workspace

### Experiment 3: Leakage pressure-not that essential, but we can do it for stronger analysis

To measure how much weight a saturated product can withstand before leaking

Saturated pad form experiment 1 is needed

Procedure:

1.  Put on gloves
2.  Saturate product with 10 mL of simulated fluid (pour onto center)
3.  Place flat board on the desk.
4.  Put a book under one edge (tilt ~10–15°)
5.  Place dry paper towels at the lower edge of the board
6.  Place saturated product on board, with its lower edge touching the paper towel
7.  Add a small weight (e.g., 100–200 g) gently onto the center of the product/pad
8.  Wait for 5 seconds. Check if fluid has reached the paper towel
9.  If there is no leak, add more weight (increase by 100–200 g each time)
10. Repeat until fluid first appears on the paper towel
11. Record total mass (grams) of all weights added at that moment in Table 3
12. Dispose of used product in Ziploc bag (or anything else)
13. Repeat steps 2–11 for all products (skip tampon, not possible)
14. Repeat 3 times per product for an average (but with new saturated pad)
15. Clean workspace
